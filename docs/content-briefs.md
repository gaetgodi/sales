# godindev.com — Content Briefs (Working Document)

**Purpose of this document:** for each page, what it needs to accomplish, what it should cover, and what it should deliberately avoid — not the final copy itself. This is the brief we write *from* when we sit down to draft actual page content.

---

## Home

**Job of this page:** make a visitor understand in ten seconds what kind of developer this is and why that matters to them, then point them clearly toward the right next page.

**Must include:**
- The tagline, front and center: "Understand the business. Then build the software." (rotating variants per Section 21 of the plan)
- A short statement of the core positioning: 50+ years, one developer, understanding-first approach — not a features list, a philosophy statement
- Three or four "signposts" — one line each pointing toward Services, Work, About, Event Photography, Contact — so a visitor self-selects toward what they actually want rather than reading everything

**Tone:** confident, plain-spoken, not salesy. This page earns trust by being direct, not by making claims.

**Avoid:** anything that reads like a generic agency homepage ("we build innovative solutions"). No technical incident details. No mention of AI as a headline feature — it belongs in Approach/About, referenced briefly here at most.

## Services

**Job of this page:** let three different kinds of visitor each find their own path within seconds — Recipes (Model A), Custom Development & Hosting (Model B), Legacy Site Migration (Model B) — using the per-page secondary nav from Section 19.

**Sub-section: Custom Development & Hosting**
- Lead with the free-discovery, paid-after-plan model (Section 22) — this is a genuine differentiator, say it plainly
- Mention the premise-visit / understanding-first approach concretely, not abstractly
- Note limited capacity — "a few clients at a time" framing (Section 26) — without sounding falsely scarce
- Soft confidence line about taking on projects others gave up on (Section 16) — not the specific payment mechanic, just the invitation to talk

**Sub-section: Legacy Site Migration**
- Name the pain directly: Drupal/Joomla getting harder to maintain, harder to meet client expectations with
- Fixed fee, hosting included in the ongoing service agreement, domain transfer optional (Section 12)
- One line on the undocumented-system-reconstruction strength (Section 14) — brief, confident, not a war story

**Sub-section: Recipes**
- The three paths from Section 6: hosted service, licensed product, individual/family site
- Link or point to the live sandbox demo
- Should feel lighter and more product-y than the other two sub-sections — this is the one thing a stranger can just try

**Avoid:** blending Model A and Model B language — they should read as clearly different kinds of engagement, per Section 1.

## About

**Job of this page:** carry the full weight of the Approach philosophy (Section 20) and the personal story that supports it — this is where depth belongs.

**Must include:**
- The core thesis, expanded from Home's brief version: "software only helps if it fits how the business actually works," and why that's harder than the code itself
- The hiring philosophy — valuing a feel for the business over raw technical skill — as a natural bridge into how AI is used today (Section 20's framing: same judgment, new tool)
- A dedicated Approach section (Section 19's decision: within About, linked from the footer)
- 50+ years, sole developer, Godin London Incorporated — the actual professional facts, stated plainly

**Optional, worth a decision when drafting:** how much of the specific personal detail (astrophotography, RASC membership, fly fishing) belongs here versus staying entirely private. A little personality can support the "real person, real relationship" positioning — worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to none or too much.

**Avoid:** turning this into a resume. It should read as "here's how I think," not "here's what I've done" — Work/Case Studies already covers the latter.

## Contact

**Job of this page:** make the first move easy and low-risk.

**Must include:**
- Restate the free-discovery offer plainly — this page is where a hesitant visitor gets the final reassurance to reach out
- A form that routes into FluentCRM correctly tagged by source (Section 7) — needs the actual page/section a visitor came from reflected in the tag
- Direct contact info as a fallback for anyone who'd rather not use a form

**Avoid:** a wall of required fields. Keep it to what's actually needed for a first conversation — this isn't a project intake form, it's an invitation to talk.

## Event Photography

**Job of this page:** stand as a genuinely separate, lighter offering that still reflects the same care as the rest of the site.

**Must include:**
- The offer itself: private event photography, group photo experience, photos available after the event
- Photos or a short description of the PHE '70 reunion work as proof of concept (Section 24) — this is the one page where showing rather than telling is easy, since the material already exists
- Pricing framed honestly as a starting rate (Section 24) — worth deciding at drafting time whether to state the $500/half-day figure publicly or invite a conversation instead

**Avoid:** over-explaining the technical build behind the photobooth — a visitor here wants "will this be fun and will I get good photos," not a description of the MediaPipe pipeline.
